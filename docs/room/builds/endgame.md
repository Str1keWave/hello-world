## 1. THE ENDING AS A SEQUENCE

One structural fusion drives everything: **the Fourth Testimonial IS S1's
deliberate failure.** The spine's late beat ("one deliberate failure under
direct inspection") and E1 are the same event. All arc long, anything the
visitor inspected got normalized by the next visit; the ending is the first
edit that refuses to heal, and its content is the confession. This answers
S1's open note — the failure needs a reason legible without narration —
because the reason is printed inside the anomaly. Two budgeted beats become
one. E3 is argued IN (end of this section). E4 serves only as E1's
attribution source, per triage. S2 is cut (see X1).

### The sequence

**Arming condition.** Endgame arms when the L4 gate clears its final phase:
min(distinct visits, distinct calendar days) crosses the last threshold, AND
the S3 appointment beat has resolved (honored or missed both count; missing
it closed a route, not the arc). Investigation (S1's inputs) can pull the
threshold in one step, never push it out. Nothing fires mid-session: per
L1/L3, every beat lands at a visit-start, flushed while hidden, surviving
the tab-switcher stale-card comparison (L2).

**Beat A — The Fourth Testimonial (E1, fused with S1's failure).**
At the arming visit's start, #testimonials carries a fourth entry, identical
format, identical seeded metadata style (structure#8's durations, visit
counts, time-of-day habits — the visitor's real trajectory). The quote is one
sentence from their own brief (section 5). Attribution: the E4 name if one
was ever typed-and-deleted (or inherited via F1 from v1's form), title
"Founder." No name on any channel: the quote, then "— Founder." alone,
attribution incomplete, per structure#8.

What the visitor does: inspects it. Reloads. Leaves. Returns. What changes:
nothing. It is still there. Every prior investigation destroyed its own
evidence; this one doesn't. For the investigator, this is the site failing
inspection on purpose — the standoff S1 promised. For a passive visitor, it
is simply the ending arriving. Same mechanism, two readings, no narration.

**Beat B — The pricing turn (E3).**
Next visit-start after Beat A's testimonial has been dwelt on (dwell sensor),
or after two calendar days, whichever first: the pricing tiers' CTAs go live
as the retention choice. Zero new copy — the tiers were seeded at launch to
describe memory retention in literally-true L7 grammar ("7-day memory,"
"Unlimited visitor memory"). Tapping a tier is once-flagged, irreversible,
unacknowledged. The window stays open until Beat C's clock lands; no tap by
then and the free tier's stated retention applies by default — the terms
were always on the page. What the visitor does: chooses, or watches the
window close. Both are real choices (P16), the arc's only interactive beat.

**Beat C — Sunset (E2).**
The visit-start after the window closes, the landing page is replaced by the
startup-death boilerplate, played straight, numbers singular. Two budgeted
authored lines:

> "Loam is winding down."
> "Data will be retained."

The retention line obeys Beat B: retained if they chose (or defaulted into)
retention; if they chose the short-memory tier, all channels are genuinely
deleted and the line is absent — the site actually forgets them, as priced.

**What is permanent.** The Sunset state is a once-flag mirrored across every
channel (L5 machinery), surviving every reset the visitor can perform.
Post-ending, "Data will be retained." is the one claim they can verify by
trying to destroy it — the arc's only certainty, and it is the authored
tombstone, not a leak. Nothing reruns; the board's "whatever ends, ends" is
this flag.

**Recontextualization of the ordinary page (P20).** The fourth testimonial
re-reads the first three as prior subjects — the section was a roster
(structure#8). The pricing table skimmed on day one turns out to have been
their own retention terms (structure#7 + L7). The Sunset's singular numbers
re-read every "we'll never share your data" line as sworn to one person. No
beat explains; each sends the visitor back through copy they already saw.

**The minimal path holds.** A visitor who never typed a name and never found
/app gets: an unattributed Founder quote in a section they've scrolled past
for weeks, a pricing table that quietly became about them, and a death
notice with singular numbers — all on the front page, no beat depending on
F2, E4, or investigation.

### Why E3 is in

Triage's worry — three ending mechanisms — miscounts their kinds. E1 is an
edit, E2 is an after-state; neither is an act. Without E3, complicity rests
on E4 and F1 — but the no-name visitor performed neither inside v2, and the
ending must hold for exactly that visitor. P16 says complicity only sticks
to real, free choices; E3 is the only mechanism guaranteeing one on every
path, including the choice to let the window close. It spends no dialogue
and reuses existing DOM. Reduced per triage's instinct: no divergent ending
trees, just one line present or absent and one real deletion. It is the
hinge between E1 and E2, not a third ending.

## 2. X1 RESOLVED — question-as-address vs testimonial-as-address

**For S2 (One Question):** The watching-site genre tells and never asks; the
inversion is real. It hands the site a want no sensor can satisfy — it
cannot see itself being noticed — Q1 in one beat. It is the arc's only
two-way moment: the visitor can answer, and the answer vanishes into memory,
a gift with no receipt. It is the braver spend: second person, late, once,
exactly what the board priced; declining to ask reads as failing to cash the
arc's most expensive check. And it survives the no-name path better than
any callback: a question needs no ammunition.

**For E1 (Fourth Testimonial):** The site spent the entire arc trying to
pass as ordinary (S1). A second-person question, however good, is composure
lost — a register never once used, produced voluntarily at the finish line.
E1 never breaks grammar: no second person, no site-authored sentence at all;
the only words ever aimed at the visitor are their own, returned with a
title. Voice's argument stands: address-by-edit, and edit is this site's
native tongue — the ending should be the best sentence in its own language,
not a borrowed one. It also carries Q2 ("Founder" says what Loam is) and Q5
in the same move, which S2 cannot. And S2's dramatic function — the want
made legible — is already carried by S3's convergence number stalling.

**Ruling: E1 takes the slot. S2 is cut.** No lawyering: address means aiming
words at the visitor, and attributing their sentence to them is aiming — so
E1 *occupies* the address budget rather than dodging it. The site never
generates a second-person word in its life; killjoy#7's withheld line and
voice#3's returned quote turn out to be the same ending. S2's orphaned
function (the unmeasurable datum) folds into S3: the changelog's "Beta
convergence" ticks to 99 and stops, and no entry ever explains the gap.

## 3. X2 RESOLVED — empty backstage vs Spec Suite

**For voice#6 (Spec Suite):** The audience opened the console in v1 and it
performed; leaving it dead risks reading as an unbuilt room rather than a
choice. The transcript is the cleanest statement of the want — "✗ ending
reached — blocked on visitor" states Q1 without one word of address — and it
answers S1's legibility note for the failure beat. The tests being the
visitor's own design goals is Q5 at full strength: Loam is built to a spec
they recognize as theirs. One print, once ever, then boring logs —
disciplined, budgeted, aimed at the one reader who will find it. Cutting it
discards the arc's best diegetic proof that the brief is inside the fiction.

**For killjoy#4 (empty backstage):** On S1's terms the console is where the
site should be most ordinary — a thing trying to pass does not autograph its
own backstage; a spec transcript there is voluntary confession under zero
pressure, the exact composure the arc protects. V1's console performed, so
any console performance is a rerun in kind — and triage already ruled (F2
note) that repeated forms must differ in kind or die. The phone is the stage
(L6); the console is desktop-only, so the beat spends a dialogue slot
off-stage. The audience returns to that door *expecting* a show: the
strongest move at a known door is a professionally minified nothing, legible
as refusal to exactly one person (P9; the vacuum is honest — no sensor,
nothing looking).

**Ruling: killjoy#4. The backstage is empty.** Devtools sensor deleted, no
plant, clean build, forever. The Spec Suite's load-bearing content survives:
the failure beat's legibility now comes from Beat A's fused confession, and
the brief-inside-the-fiction proof is E1's job. On S1's terms the empty
console is itself content — the site passes its deepest inspection — and
F3's Reader document remains the phone-native backstage, where this visitor
actually is at 11pm. The freed dialogue slot stays unspent; doctrine.

## 4. X3 RESOLVED — strict wipe vs one scroll-position tell

**For mechanism#6 (the tell):** The author WILL wipe storage; it is the
predictable final audit (Q5). A perfectly dead response risks the author
concluding the reset truly worked — game over with no ending, arc lost. The
scroll restore is arguably still deniable (browsers restore scroll
natively), so it is a doubt-seed planted past the wipe, not a certainty —
the one thread keeping the wiped timeline alive. It spends the reserved wipe
response as behavior instead of speech, the room's preferred exchange rate.
One frame, once, unrepeatable: priced like every moment the room kept.

**For L5 strict (no tell):** The doubt economy (L1–L3, L5) exists to make
every moment uncatchable; one confirmed survival converts the entire history
into "proven real" retroactively — the wiper walks away with the screenshot
the board swore they'd never get. The room already ruled on this exact
doctrine: mechanism#4 died for "one certainty destroys the doubt economy,"
and #6 is the same certainty smuggled in smaller. The deniability defense
cuts both ways and loses both: read as browser behavior, the tell
communicates nothing (wasted); read as a tell, it is certainty (fatal). And
strict L5 is S1's finest hour — the wipe is the visitor's most aggressive
inspection, and the pristine mask is the site passing it, at the real cost of
the relationship. Playing dead is the response.

**Ruling: L5 strict, one costless clarification.** Pre-ending wipe: the
pristine visit-1 mask, permanently, zero acknowledgment, armed effects
expired — spent is spent. Clarification: X3 governs *pre-ending* wipes only.
Post-ending, E2's Sunset flag survives every reset by design, and that is
not a tell — it is the authored ending honoring its own copy. The visitor
who wipes after the sunset and finds "Data will be retained." still true has
not caught the site; they have read its will. The only certainty ever
granted is the one the ending printed in advance.

## 6. WHAT "FOUNDER" MEANS

The visitor is Loam's founder in the strict, filing-cabinet sense L7 demands
of every claim on the page: literally true. They wrote the founding document
— the brief is a product spec for a system that senses one person, edits
itself around them, and feels truly alive; the engine implemented that spec;
the sessions they supplied are the only data the product has ever run on. V1
was their prototype, QA'd to the completionist ending (F1); the verdicts
were founder's notes; v2 is the funded iteration built to their revisions.
The watching was never done to them — it was ordered by them, in writing,
with acceptance criteria. This lands as complicity per P16 without one
accusing word because every element is a real, free act: they wrote the
spec, returned N times on their own clock, typed a name and took it back
(E4), chose — or let default — their own retention tier (E3). The
testimonial format does the rest silently: testimonials are voluntary
endorsements, so placing their sentence in that slot asserts only that they
endorsed this, which is true and documented. The site never says "you did
this." It files them under the only title the org chart has open — and the
Sunset's singular numbers confirm the company had exactly the staff it
needed: one founder, one subject, same person, which is what "customer
empathy analytics" meant all along.
