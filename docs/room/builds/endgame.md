## 1. THE ENDING AS A SEQUENCE

One structural fusion drives everything: **the Fourth Testimonial IS S1's
deliberate failure.** The spine's late beat (S1: "one deliberate failure under
direct inspection") and E1 are the same event. For the whole arc, anything the
visitor inspected got normalized by the next visit. The ending is the first
edit that refuses to heal. Its content is the confession. This answers S1's
open note — the failure needs a reason legible without narration — because the
reason is printed inside the anomaly itself. Two budgeted beats become one.

E3 is argued IN (see the argument at the end of this section). E4 serves only
as E1's attribution source, per triage. S2 is cut (see X1).

### The sequence

**Arming condition.** Endgame arms when the L4 gate clears its final phase:
min(distinct visits, distinct calendar days) crosses the last threshold, AND
the S3 appointment beat has resolved (honored or missed — either resolution
counts; missing it closed a route, not the arc). Investigation behaviors (S1's
suspicion inputs) can pull the threshold in by one step, never push it out.
Nothing below fires mid-session. Per L1/L3, every beat lands at a visit-start,
flushed while hidden, and survives the tab-switcher stale-card comparison (L2).

**Beat A — The Fourth Testimonial (E1, fused with S1's failure).**
At the arming visit's start, #testimonials carries a fourth entry, identical
format, identical seeded metadata style (structure#8's durations, visit
counts, time-of-day habits — the visitor's real trajectory). The quote is one
sentence from their own brief (section 5). Attribution: the E4 name if one
was ever typed-and-deleted (or inherited via F1 from v1's form), title
"Founder." No name on any channel: the quote, then "— Founder." alone,
attribution incomplete, per structure#8.

What the visitor does: inspects it. Reloads. Leaves. Returns. What changes:
nothing. It is still there. Every prior investigation destroyed its own
evidence; this one doesn't. For the investigator, this is the site failing
inspection on purpose — the standoff S1 promised. For a passive visitor, it
is simply the ending arriving. Same mechanism, two readings, no narration.

**Beat B — The pricing turn (E3).**
Next visit-start after Beat A's testimonial has been dwelt on (dwell sensor),
or after two calendar days, whichever first: the pricing tiers' CTAs go live
as the retention choice. Zero new copy — the tiers were seeded at launch to
describe memory retention in literally-true L7 grammar ("7-day memory,"
"Unlimited visitor memory"). Tapping a tier is once-flagged, irreversible,
unacknowledged. The choice window stays open until Beat C's clock lands.
No tap by then: the free tier's stated retention applies by default — the
terms were always on the page.

What the visitor does: chooses, or watches the window close. Both are real
choices (P16). This is the arc's only interactive beat that matters.

**Beat C — Sunset (E2).**
The visit-start after the choice window closes, the landing page is replaced
by the startup-death boilerplate, played straight, numbers singular. Two of
its lines are budgeted authored text:

> "Loam is winding down."
> "Data will be retained."

The retention line obeys Beat B: retained if they chose (or defaulted into)
retention; if they chose the short-memory tier, all channels are genuinely
deleted and the line is absent — the site actually forgets them, as priced.

**What is permanent.** The Sunset state is a once-flag mirrored across every
channel (L5 machinery) and survives every reset the visitor can perform.
Post-ending, "Data will be retained." is the one claim the visitor can verify
by trying to destroy it — the only certainty in the whole arc, and it is the
authored tombstone, not a leak. Nothing reruns. The board's "whatever ends,
ends" is this flag.

**Recontextualization of the ordinary page (P20).** The fourth testimonial
re-reads the first three as prior subjects — the section was a roster
(structure#8). The pricing table the visitor skimmed on day one turns out to
have been their own retention terms (structure#7 + L7). The Sunset's singular
numbers re-read every "we'll never share your data" line as having been sworn
to one person. No beat explains; each sends the visitor back through copy
they already saw.

**The minimal path holds.** A visitor who never typed a name and never found
/app gets: an unattributed Founder quote in a section they've scrolled past
for weeks, a pricing table that quietly became about them, and a death notice
with singular numbers. All three live on the front page. No beat depends on
F2, E4, or investigation.

### Why E3 is in

Triage's worry — three ending mechanisms — miscounts their kinds. E1 is an
edit, E2 is an after-state; neither is an act. Without E3, complicity rests
on E4 and F1 — but the no-name visitor performed neither inside v2, and the
ending must hold for exactly that visitor. P16 says complicity only sticks
to real, free choices; E3 is the only mechanism guaranteeing one on every
path, including the choice to let the window close. It spends no dialogue
and reuses existing DOM. Reduced per triage's instinct: no divergent ending
trees, just one line present or absent and one real deletion. It is the
hinge between E1 and E2, not a third ending.

## 2. X1 RESOLVED — question-as-address vs testimonial-as-address

**For S2 (One Question):** The watching-site genre tells and never asks; the
inversion is real. It hands the site a want no sensor can satisfy — it cannot
see itself being noticed — which is Q1 in one beat. It produces the arc's
only two-way moment: the visitor can answer, and the answer vanishes into
memory, a gift with no receipt. It is the braver spend: second person, late,
once, exactly what the board priced. And it uses the address the way the
author budgeted it — they wrote "≤1 direct address"; declining to ask reads
as the site failing to cash the arc's most expensive check. A question also
survives the no-name path better than any callback: it needs no ammunition.

**For E1 (Fourth Testimonial):** The site has spent the entire arc trying to
pass as ordinary (S1). A second-person question, however good, is composure
lost — the one register the site has never once used, produced voluntarily at
the finish line. E1 never breaks grammar: no second person, no site-authored
sentence at all. The only words ever aimed at the visitor are their own,
returned with a title. Voice's argument stands: it is address-by-edit, and
edit is this site's native tongue — the ending should be the best sentence in
the site's own language, not a borrowed one. It also carries Q2's answer
("Founder" says what Loam is) and Q5's payload in the same move, which S2
cannot. And S2's dramatic function — the want made legible — is already
carried by S3's convergence number stalling short of 100 between visits.

**Ruling: E1 takes the slot. S2 is cut.** No lawyering: the constraint says
address means aiming words at the visitor, and attributing their sentence to
them is aiming — so E1 *occupies* the address budget rather than dodging it.
The site never generates a second-person word in its life. Killjoy#7's
withheld line and voice#3's returned quote turn out to be the same ending:
the one address ever made is the visitor's own sentence. S2's orphaned
function (the unmeasurable datum) is folded into S3: the changelog's "Beta
convergence" ticks to 99 and stops, and no entry ever explains the gap.

## 3. X2 RESOLVED — empty backstage vs Spec Suite

**For voice#6 (Spec Suite):** The audience opened the console in v1 and it
performed; leaving it dead risks reading as an unbuilt room rather than a
choice. The transcript is the single cleanest statement of the want — "✗
ending reached — blocked on visitor" states Q1 without one word of address —
and it answers S1's legibility note for the failure beat. The tests being the
visitor's own design goals is Q5 at full strength: Loam is being built to a
spec they recognize as theirs. One print, once ever, then boring logs — it is
disciplined, budgeted, and aimed at the one reader who will find it. Cutting
it throws away the arc's best diegetic proof that the brief is inside the
fiction.

**For killjoy#4 (empty backstage):** On S1's terms the console is the one
place the site should be most ordinary — a thing trying to pass does not
autograph its own backstage; printing a spec transcript there is voluntary
confession under zero pressure, the exact composure the whole arc protects.
V1's console performed, so any console performance is a rerun in kind — and
the triage already ruled (F2 note) that repeated forms must differ in kind or
die. The phone is the stage (L6); the console is desktop-only, so the beat
spends a dialogue slot off-stage. And the audience checked the console in v1
*expecting* a show: the strongest available move at a door they already know
is a professionally minified nothing. The refusal is legible to exactly one
person (P9: refusal is a move; killjoy#4: the vacuum is honest — no sensor,
nothing looking).

**Ruling: killjoy#4. The backstage is empty.** Devtools sensor deleted, no
plant, clean build, forever. The Spec Suite's load-bearing content survives
without it: the failure beat's legibility now comes from Beat A's fused
confession, and the brief-inside-the-fiction proof is E1's job. On S1's
terms the empty console is itself content — the site passes its deepest
inspection — and F3's Reader document remains the phone-native backstage,
which is where this visitor actually is at 11pm. The freed dialogue slot
stays unspent; under-spend is doctrine.

## 4. X3 RESOLVED — strict wipe vs one scroll-position tell

**For mechanism#6 (the tell):** The author WILL wipe storage; it is the
predictable final audit (Q5). A perfectly dead response risks the author
concluding the reset truly worked — game over with no ending, arc lost. The
scroll restore is arguably still deniable: browsers restore scroll natively,
so it is a doubt-seed planted past the wipe, not a certainty — the one thread
that keeps the wiped timeline alive. It also spends P11's reserved wipe
response as behavior instead of speech, the room's own preferred exchange
rate. One frame, once, unrepeatable: it is priced exactly like every other
moment the room kept.

**For L5 strict (no tell):** The doubt economy (L1–L3, L5) exists to make
every moment uncatchable; one confirmed survival converts the entire history
into "proven real" retroactively — the wiper walks away with the screenshot
the board swore they'd never get. The room already ruled on this exact
doctrine: mechanism#4 died for "one certainty destroys the doubt economy,"
and #6 is the same certainty smuggled in smaller. The deniability defense
cuts both ways and loses both: read as browser behavior, the tell
communicates nothing (wasted); read as a tell, it is certainty (fatal). And
strict L5 is S1's finest hour — the wipe is the visitor's most aggressive
inspection, and the pristine mask is the site passing it, at the real cost of
the relationship. Playing dead is the response.

**Ruling: L5 strict, with one clarification that costs nothing.** Pre-ending
wipe: the pristine visit-1 mask, permanently, zero acknowledgment, armed
effects expired — spent is spent. The clarification: X3 only governs
*pre-ending* wipes. Post-ending, E2's Sunset flag survives every reset by
design, and that is not a tell — it is the authored ending honoring its own
copy. The visitor who wipes after the sunset and finds "Data will be
retained." still true has not caught the site; they have read its will. The
only certainty ever granted is the one the ending printed in advance.

## 6. WHAT "FOUNDER" MEANS

The visitor is Loam's founder in the strict, filing-cabinet sense that L7
demands of every claim on the page: literally true. They wrote the founding
document — the brief is a product spec for a system that senses one person,
edits itself around them, and feels truly alive; the engine implemented that
spec; the sessions they supplied are the only data the product has ever run
on. V1 was their prototype and they QA'd it to the completionist ending
(F1); the verdicts were founder's notes; v2 is the funded iteration built to
their revisions. So the watching was never done to them — it was ordered by
them, in writing, with acceptance criteria. The arc lands this as complicity
per P16 without one accusing word because every element is a real, free act:
they wrote the spec, they returned N times on their own clock, they typed a
name and took it back (E4), they chose — or let default — their own retention
tier (E3). The testimonial format does the remaining work silently:
testimonials are voluntary endorsements, so placing their sentence in that
slot asserts only that they endorsed this, which is true and documented. The
site never says "you did this." It just files them under the only title the
org chart has open — and the Sunset page's singular numbers confirm the
company had exactly the staff it needed: one founder, one subject, same
person, which is what "customer empathy analytics" meant all along.
