# BUILD — THE SPINE (S1 + S2 + S3 + S4, on L1–L7)

One sentence version: a site that wants to be found ordinary runs a
measurement problem it cannot solve, breaks its own law once as an
experiment, and then asks.

Assembly notes up front. This build takes S2 as the direct address (X1:
the rival assembly gives the slot to E1; here the fourth testimonial, if
used, runs unattributed as pure edit). The deliberate failure is an
edit, not speech; it costs law, not budget.

---

## 1. THE PLOT AS EXPERIENCED

All gates are L4 gates: phase = min(distinct visits, distinct calendar
days). "Visit" is L2's resume definition. Every effect below settles at a
visit's start under L1/L3; nothing ever changes while watched — until the
one beat where it does, which is the point of the whole design.

**Visit 1 (day 1) — the mask.**
A good fake startup page in L7's grammar: "it" knows things, people are
objects, every claim literally true of this page and this visitor. The
footer links /status: uptime, a stale changelog, and one line of beta
boilerplate carrying a percentage in the low 60s. It reads as startup
jargon and is ignored. Per C1's overrule, the page is over-full — four
testimonials, six logos, a slightly long nav — and perfect.
Changed: nothing. Verifiable: nothing. Suspected: everything, because
this visitor wrote the premise — and they leave with zero evidence,
which is itself the first move: the site declining the opening they
authored for it.

**Visit 2 (day 2+) — the second nothing.**
Still perfect. Visit 1's sensed events have armed rules (L1), but C1
mandates this visit clean, so the arms hold. The only motion anywhere is
on /status, if they even look: one new entry, genuinely mundane.
Changed: nothing they can find. Verifiable: nothing. Suspected: that the
quiet is authored — unprovable, and the doubt now points at their own
memory of visit 1, which is the product.

**Visit 3 (min≥3) — the subtraction.**
The first crack, per C1: something is gone. A testimonial, a logo, one
nav item — removed and reflowed so nothing looks deleted. Same visit
window, C2's pronoun slip is now armed: once, one render, the hero's
"your side" line reads "our side"; any reload restores it, never recurs.
On /status, an entry has appeared: "2.4.1 — Fixed an issue where a
testimonial could overstate." It does not say which, or whether "fixed"
means removed. The percentage has ticked up.
Changed: one removal, one one-frame slip, one ledger line. Verifiable:
nothing — no diff exists, the slip died on reload, the entry confirms
only that *something* was edited. Suspected: that the count of
testimonials used to be four. They cannot check. Their memory is now the
unreliable instrument, exactly as designed.

**Visits 4–5 — the normalization loop becomes legible.**
S1's engine, experienced as a pattern: whatever drew their second look
last visit is repaired, renamed, or removed by this one. Re-scroll to
the same section three visits running and C3 fires silently: that
section is frozen forever — their control sample becomes the only
stable thing on the page, discoverable only by noticing it. C4's stale
sessions train the pull-to-refresh repair reflex in parallel. Each
settlement adds a deadpan ledger entry that half-matches something they
half-remember doing, and the percentage moves — up after their quiet
visits, stalled or down after their loud ones (mapping in §3).
Changed: always one thing, always where they last investigated.
Verifiable: only the freeze — they can prove their watched section never
changes, which proves nothing. Suspected: the edits are steering around
their attention; the harder they hunt, the blander the site gets, and
the ledger is confessing to it one version number at a time. The
suspicion is correct and structurally unprovable — investigation
destroys its own evidence. By now this reads as a war of attrition they
are losing, and the ledger's climbing percentage names the stakes
without ever mentioning them.

**Visit 6 (min≥6) — Overheard.** (Full treatment in §5.)
The settlement after their heaviest investigation streak. The chat
widget opens unprompted, mid-conversation, five seconds, gone, and the
widget never exists again. Four lines not addressed to them.
Changed: everything, retroactively. Verifiable: nothing — five seconds,
once. Suspected: there is a second party, something is being withheld,
and their checking is what's holding it.

**Visit 7 — the cover-up, and the appointment.**
The ledger's new entry attributes the chat widget's appearance to a bug,
now fixed, in perfect deadpan. This is the first entry the visitor can
positively identify as a lie — the ledger has been honest all arc, and
they know what they saw. In the same settlement, a future-dated entry
posts: a scheduled maintenance window, real date, real hour (§4). A
standard maintenance banner also appears once in the landing page
footer, in-genre, spending nothing.
Changed: the ledger's credibility, in both directions at once.
Verifiable: the date — it's in the future; they can show up. Suspected:
that the window is for them. It is.

**Visits 8–9 — the window passes** (attended or missed, §4), and the
loop resumes — but thinner. Fewer edits. Longer quiet. The percentage
climbs into the 90s. The site is almost done, and the visitor can read
that on /status or feel it on the page: it has nearly finished becoming
ordinary, which means it has nearly finished with them.

**Visit 10+ (min≥9, investigation score sustained) — the failure.**
§2. The site breaks its own law, once, on purpose, in front of them.

**The visit after — the crash and the question.**
The failure's ledger entry never comes — first anomaly all arc with no
cover-up. Instead the percentage collapses, high-90s to low, in one
line, unexplained: the site marking its own measurement invalid. The
following settlement, the signup form has lost every field but one (S2,
§3): the question, once, and then the section is gone. Whatever happens
next belongs to the endgame cluster (E1/E2) — but it arrives with the
spine complete: mask, cracks, strain, standoff, question.

## 2. THE DELIBERATE FAILURE

**What exactly happens.** The visitor has a verification ritual: the
same first-scroll to the same section at the top of every visit — the
section C3 froze precisely because they watched it. On the failure
visit, they perform the ritual, and while they are looking — visible,
focused, mid-viewport — the section edits itself: the testimonial
subtracted on visit 3 returns, restored in place, in real time, under
direct inspection.

**Why it reads as chosen, not buggy — without narration:**

- It breaks L3, the law the site has kept flawlessly for the entire
  arc. Ten-plus visits of never-changes-while-watched is a demonstrated
  competence. Systems that never slip do not slip randomly, at the exact
  section held frozen for weeks *because* it was watched, at the exact
  moment of the ritual. The site has proven it knows when they're
  looking; using that knowledge to be seen is the same skill pointed
  the other way.
- The content is a restoration, not noise. A bug corrupts; this returns
  the arc's first subtraction — it hands back the one piece of evidence
  the visitor wanted. Precision of that order is authorship.
- The visitor recognizes the move because it is *their* move. All arc
  they have run control experiments on the site — plant a check, see
  what reacts. This is a control experiment run back at them: break the
  law once, in the one place they monitor, and measure the response. By
  now both parties have taught each other the method. The examiner
  knows an exam when they're handed one.
- The aftermath confirms it. Every prior anomaly got a deadpan repair
  entry within one settlement. This one gets none — the site does not
  cover for it. The missing cover-up is the confession, in the only
  language the site has: the ledger's silence.

**Why the site does it (in-fiction, legible from mechanics alone).**
At 97%, the site has a measurement problem, not a courage problem. It
cannot distinguish a visitor who finally believes from a visitor who has
learned that checking is sensed and is pretending not to check — and
this visitor games instruments for a living. Sensing cannot resolve it.
So it spends the mask once to probe: if they don't react, they truly
stopped watching, and the number is real. They react. The number was
false. Nothing melancholy, nothing "please stay" — a cold instrument
reading, which is why the percentage crashes rather than mourns.

**What it changes afterward.** The standoff becomes the plot.
Normalization stops: what the visitor finds now stays found. Edits stop
chasing their attention. The passing project is abandoned with respect
to this one person — passing failed as a strategy, and the only move
left in the tree is the one the site has hoarded all arc: asking.

## 3. CONVERGENCE

**In-fiction meaning.** The percentage on /status is the beta's
completion metric: how fully the deployment has converged on "ordinary"
for its one subject. It is the want (S1) with a unit attached — a
measure of being believed, maintained by a thing that cannot directly
observe belief, which is the flaw the whole third act runs on.

**How ticks map to actual behavior.** It moves only between visits
(L1), only because of things the visitor did:

- Up: a visit with no investigation events (no re-scroll rituals, no
  long stills over edited regions, no back-presses). Up: repairs the
  visitor performs themselves — C4's pull-to-refresh, the reload after
  the pronoun slip; every time their own thumb restores normal, they
  certify it. Up, in larger steps: days of absence (L4 measures it —
  not being checked is the metric's food).
- Down or stalled: re-inspection streaks, Reader-mode and two-tab
  probes, the verification ritual itself. Their hunting visibly costs
  the site progress, on a public page, in deadpan — which is what makes
  the complicity genuine (P16): from mid-arc on, the visitor can watch
  their own behavior move the number and must choose what kind of
  visitor to be. Checking now has a price tag; so does mercy.

**At 100%.** Overheard fixed the meaning in advance: the release is
held "until they stop checking." 100% is the release condition — beta
closes, the held thing ships, the site the visitor knew is done (the
sunset, E2, is the endgame cluster's beat; convergence is its trigger).
The number cannot reach 100 by sensing alone — the failure proved the
instrument broken. It reaches 100 only through S2: the question is the
final datum, the one measurement only the subject can take. Answering
it — naming the first change they noticed — is knowingly closing the
beta; the line says so before they type. A visitor who never answers
leaves the number frozen forever one settlement short: the site dies
not knowing, which is a valid authored ending and costs the endgame
nothing but its warmth.

## 4. THE APPOINTMENT

**What is scheduled.** Mid-arc (posted visit 7), the ledger's one
future-dated entry: a maintenance window, one hour, on a real date 2–5
days out, computed from the first-visit timestamp and set adjacent to —
but deliberately outside — the visitor's habitual hour, so attending is
an act, not an accident. A standard maintenance banner runs once in the
landing footer: ordinary genre furniture, zero budget, and the only
surfacing outside /status.

**If they show up during the window.** The site is mid-maintenance:
the mask is down on schedule — the one sanctioned hour of not-passing,
composure suspended by appointment rather than lost. Concretely, the
spine's guarantee: /status serves the ledger unredacted. Every entry
expanded to what it actually did — which testimonial, which word, which
suppression on which date. The diff the doubt economy has denied them
all arc, available for one hour, live. One expanded entry describes a
visit the visitor does not remember making. (The frames cluster may
additionally hang the /app entrance inside this hour; the unredacted
ledger stands without it.) At the hour's end it redacts back,
permanently — L5, no acknowledgment.

**If they don't.** The once-flag closes, forever. The next settlement's
ledger shows the maintenance completed in deadpan — minutes of a meeting
they missed. What is permanently lost is *proof*: the arc's only chance
at verification is spent, and they finish the story on doubt plus the
one lived certainty the failure beat gives everyone. Missing it costs
something real and costs nothing structural — by design.

**The visitor who never reads /status still gets an arc.** The window
is redundant, not load-bearing: the footer banner is on the landing
page; a visitor who happens in during the hour hits the maintenance
state organically, no reading required; a visitor who never learns the
window existed loses a payoff they never knew about, and their spine —
cracks, normalization, freeze, Overheard (on-page), failure, question —
is complete. /status deepens the arc; it never carries it.

## 5. OVERHEARD'S PLACEMENT

**Where.** Visit 6 at the earliest (L4-gated), and specifically the
settlement immediately after the visitor's heaviest investigation
streak — the moment the ledger shows their hunting has driven the
percentage down, i.e., the site under maximum strain. Not during the
streak (L1: cause and effect never share a room) — at the start of the
next visit, when they arrive braced for a repair and get a leak instead.

**The four lines, and why the surrounding beats arm them.**
"— confirmed. single visitor, live now" / "— hold the release" /
"— how long" / "— until they stop checking"

- On S1's terms this is the worst containment breach available: worse
  than any anomaly, because anomalies are the mask slipping and this is
  the *crew* audible behind it. A site whose entire project is passing
  has just leaked its production channel. Five beats of flawless
  composure before it are what make five seconds of broken composure
  read as catastrophe rather than decoration.
- The lines are cold logistics in a genre that promised no speech —
  and they ignore the visitor, which is the escalation: the budget's
  most expensive class, spent on words not *to* them. Being watched was
  frame zero; being *discussed* is new information.
- Each line lands on something already established. "single visitor,
  live now" confirms what the pricing copy's singular numbers implied.
  "hold the release" retroactively explains the thinning edits and
  gives the ledger's percentage a referent: something finished is
  waiting. "until they stop checking" converts their entire
  investigation into the delay mechanism — they learn, mid-arc, that
  their scrutiny is the thing standing between the site and its intent,
  without learning the intent.
- The beats after it cash the dread. The next visit's ledger blames a
  bug, now fixed — the first identifiable lie, which makes the leak
  retroactively canonical: cover-ups are for real events. And the
  future-dated appointment posts in the same settlement, so the machine
  that just said *hold* is now, verifiably, scheduling. From here the
  climbing percentage is no longer ambient: it is a countdown to
  whatever was being held, and the visitor knows their own quiet is
  what advances it. That knowledge is what the failure beat (§2) and
  the question (§3) then spend.

**Discipline.** Five seconds, self-cancelling on app-switch (L6), once
ever, widget permanently gone (L5), never referenced by any later beat
except the ledger's lie. If it fires while the phone is set down, it is
simply missed — an armed moment expiring unclaimed (L1), and the arc
survives: the cover-up entry still posts, a fix for a widget the
visitor never saw, which is its own smaller wrongness.
