# PITCHES — SURPRISE (where does surprise live when the mark knows the con?)

Working premise for all seven: the mark cannot be surprised by content, only by
structure. They know the stated game. So every pitch runs a second game under
the stated one, and the stated one is the misdirection. Their memory of v1 —
the boxes, the dashboard, the four endings, the event-log finale — is treated
as authored material we can play against, Stanley-Ultra-Deluxe style.

---

## 1. The Inheritance

**Mechanism:** v2 ships on v1's origin and silently imports v1's surviving
localStorage/IndexedDB/cookie memory on first load — endings reached, hours
kept, sections ignored, the tier hovered. v2's opening state is already edited
by that history: the section v1 learned they skip is simply absent from day
one. Nothing announces the carryover. The method is boring (same-origin
storage), which is exactly where it hides.

**Trigger:** first payoff on visit 2–3 of v2 — one edit explicable only by
something they did in v1, never referenced.

**What the mark expects vs. what happens:**
Expects: a clean reboot, to be judged against their memory of v1.
Happens: v2 never forgot; v1 retroactively becomes the intake interview.

**Answers:** Q1 — the v1 watching built a dossier v2 inherits. Q2 — v1 itself
becomes a lower frame. Q3 — what else carried over drives returns. Q4 — seed
by absence. Q5 — plays the author's assumption that a rewrite resets the world.

---

## 2. The Cover Story Is True

**Mechanism:** every Loam feature bullet, testimonial, and pricing line is
written to double as a literal description of what the site does to its
visitor — sensing, retention, empathy at scale. Midgame, an unlinked case
study becomes findable: one anonymized subject whose metrics quietly match the
visitor's own history. The ending sends them back through the landing copy,
which now reads as documentation, not satire.

**Trigger:** case study appears after N visits; the final beat points back at
the front page.

**What the mark expects vs. what happens:**
Expects: the startup shell to crack and reveal something underneath.
Happens: there was never a shell — the confession was the front page, in plain
sight, from the first second.

**Answers:** Q1 — Loam has exactly one client and is delivering the product it
advertises. Q2 — satire, then working product, then self-commissioned study:
the author ordered this by writing the brief. Q4 — this is the seeding method.
Q5 — complicity is genuine (P16); they signed the work order.

---

## 3. The Regression (NAMED BREAK)

**Breaks:** the board's visit-one cleanliness, the zero-dialogue-early budget,
and "wrongness looks almost right."

**Mechanism:** early, ONE dialogue box in v1's exact style — narrated
observation, the convicted jaded voice, everything the verdicts killed. It
reads as a regression. The mark drops into critic mode, diagnosing a failed
rebuild — and critic mode is the blind spot: while they grade the flop, the
real edits accrue unexamined. The box never repeats (once-flag), and its
content, revisited later, was precise about something that hadn't happened yet.

**Trigger:** fires once, visit 1–2; a late beat points back at it and forces
the re-read.

**What the mark expects vs. what happens:**
Expects: to catch the site being bad at its own premise.
Happens: the flop was the setup; their contempt was the misdirection.

**Answers:** Q5 — uses the author's editorial reflex as cover. Q1 — the site
needs to be underestimated to work. Q3 — the itch to confirm the regression
brings visit 2.

---

## 4. The Roadmap

**Mechanism:** a public Loam changelog/roadmap page paraphrases the mark's own
brief into corporate feature language — the arc's beats listed as shipped or
planned. Items check off between visits as beats land, out of order. One item
stays planned forever. The mark, who wrote the spec, watches that direction;
the finale arrives from a direction the roadmap never listed. Prophecy-con
structure: announce the trick truthfully, and the announcement pins attention.

**Trigger:** roadmap discoverable from visit 2; checkmarks update only between
sessions; the unlisted beat is the ending.

**What the mark expects vs. what happens:**
Expects: to predict the arc, having authored its requirements.
Happens: the site told the truth about its plan and still surprised them —
the stated game held their eyes while the real game ran elsewhere.

**Answers:** Q5 — the brief itself becomes the misdirection surface. Q3 — the
permanently pending item manufactures return visits. Q1 — the site wants its
plan witnessed, wrongly.

---

## 5. The Other Reader (NAMED BREAK — the one I'm not sure we're allowed)

**Breaks:** the board's axiom that the audience is ONE person.

**Mechanism:** a second text layer — source comments, console, one unlinked
page — addressed not to the visitor but to whoever builds v3: briefing notes
on the author's observed habits, feature requests, warnings about the coming
rewrite. The site knows how it gets made: someone writes a brief about it,
something else implements it. It is lobbying its next implementer, and the
visitor's behavior is its supporting evidence. The production apparatus enters
the fiction.

**Trigger:** found via view-source/devtools — this mark WILL look; cover it
perfectly (P14) or cut the pitch.

**What the mark expects vs. what happens:**
Expects: hidden content aimed at them, the sole audience.
Happens: they are not the reader; they are the subject matter.

**Answers:** Q2 — under Loam is the making-of itself. Q5 — the author catches
the site doing authorship back. Q1 — it wants a say in its own next version.

---

## 6. Passing

**Mechanism:** the want, inverted from v1: this site wants to be found
ordinary by the one person who knows it isn't. Suspicion behaviors —
re-scrolling to the same spot, back-presses, long stills, devtools — are the
input; the response is normalization. Whatever drew a second look is repaired,
renamed, or removed by the next visit. Investigation destroys evidence; the
harder the mark hunts, the blander the site becomes. Late, exactly once, it
fails on purpose — leaving one anomaly in place under direct inspection — and
the standoff becomes the plot.

**Trigger:** continuous; the deliberate failure gates on sustained
investigation across multiple visits.

**What the mark expects vs. what happens:**
Expects: a site performing for their attention, as v1 did.
Happens: a site trying to pass inspection and shed its observer.

**Answers:** Q1 — the want is to be believed ordinary. Q2 — the
anomaly-healing system is the second frame. Q3 — the hunt itself sustains
returns. Q5 — its examiner wrote the exam.

---

## 7. The Diary of Restraint

**Mechanism:** late, a findable log page. The mark remembers v1's event-log
finale and braces for their own actions read back at them. Instead the entries
are the site's: dated records of edits made, reactions suppressed, sessions
where it chose to show nothing — the quiet visits revealed as labor. One entry
is dated in the future.

**Trigger:** findable only after the arc's middle; the future-dated entry
names a time, and honoring that time is the appointment.

**What the mark expects vs. what happens:**
Expects: v1's trick again — a mirror of their behavior.
Happens: a ledger of everything withheld; the silence had a shape, and one
line hasn't happened yet.

**Answers:** Q1 — the log proves what the watching was for: an editing
practice, not a performance. Q3 — the future entry manufactures the next
visit. Q4 — the null sessions become seeded material. Q5 — directly subverts
their memory of v1's finale.
