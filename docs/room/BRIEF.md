# WRITERS' ROOM BRIEF — v2 of "The Site That Notices"

## What exists (v1, live now)

A fake startup landing page (Loam, "customer empathy analytics") with a full
sensing engine: every tap/scroll/selection/idle/tab-switch/back-press/reload/
devtools/return-visit flows through an event bus into a rules engine
(priority, once, cooldown, phase gating), with localStorage/cookie/IndexedDB
memory, 5 phases, 4 endings. 406 reaction rules. The sensing works.

## The audience's verdict (verbatim themes — treat as law)

1. **Overtriggering.** "I got like 15 boxes queued up when I wasn't really
   doing anything notable." Dialogue fired constantly. Almost nothing should
   trigger dialogue. Most reactions should not be dialogue at all.
2. **Too on the nose.** The site SAYS what it noticed ("You scrolled back up
   just now"). Telling, not showing. The site should EDIT ITSELF and BEHAVE
   differently instead of narrating its observations.
3. **Voice too jaded / cliché.** The melancholy lonely-AI-with-KPI-feelings
   thing read as boring and predictable.
4. **The reveal took 2 minutes and was lame.** No coherent, evolving plot —
   just reactive quips to each little thing. Pacing must be slow, over real
   time and multiple visits, reacting to things that MATTER.
5. **The audience already knows the premise.** This is built for one person
   who wrote the concept themselves. "Everything I already know is everything
   I already know." Surprise must come from somewhere else than "the site is
   watching you" — they arrive knowing that.
6. **Repetition after the ending.** Re-exploring showed the same dialogue
   again. Content must not visibly repeat; quiet is better than reruns.
7. **"It should feel truly alive, not just pretending to be."** Show, don't
   tell. "You are an AI that can program nearly anything. So be creative and
   program interesting stuff."

## Hard constraints

- Mobile-first (phone is the stage). Static site, all client-side. The
  existing sensing engine, memory channels, and deploy pipeline stay.
- No emoji, no zaniness, no horror clichés (those rules survived contact).
- Rarity is a feature: one great moment beats forty adequate ones.

## What the room must produce

Pitches. Specific, buildable, surprising. Mechanisms over monologues.
The showrunner will keep the best 20% and kill the rest without mercy.
