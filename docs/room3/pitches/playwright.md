# PLAYWRIGHT — The Shape of the Session

Five structures. Each is an arc powered only by what the visitor does. No clocks anywhere; every threshold is crossed by an action, never by elapsed time.

---

## Pitch 1 — The Taming

**The movements.** (1) MEET: the thing flinches from touch; the visitor learns gentleness is the only verb that works. (2) RITUAL: the visitor's own repeated gesture — whatever they happened to do twice — becomes "the greeting." The site canonizes it. Doing the ritual advances; novelty is tolerated, the ritual is answered. (3) CLOSER: each completed ritual earns one degree of approach — it comes nearer the glass, mirrors your rhythm, initiates once.

**The midpoint turn.** It performs YOUR ritual at YOU, unprompted. The tame becomes mutual: you realize you have also been tamed.

**The climax.** "Talking" is the ritual with one element changed — a question phrased in the grammar of your shared habit. You can answer only by gesture.

**The ending.** It teaches you a leaving-ritual. Performing it is the goodbye; the screen closes the distance one last time, then rests.

---

## Pitch 2 — The Language Lesson (Arrival shape)

**The movements.** (1) NOISE: it emits patterned signals — pulses, shapes — meaningless. Touch interrupts them; it notices interruption. (2) PIDGIN: call-and-response. It offers a signal, you echo by gesture; each confirmed pair becomes a shared word, kept visibly (a growing constellation, not a menu). Vocabulary size, not time, gates escalation. (3) SYNTAX: it starts combining your shared words into two-word utterances you can actually parse, because you built both halves.

**The midpoint turn.** You realize the words it chose to teach were never random: assembled, they describe YOU — the toucher, the fast one, the one who returns to the corner. It has been talking about you all along, in the language you thought you were teaching it.

**The climax.** Its first full sentence — in the pidgin, untranslated, and you understand it cold. The reader does the final decoding themselves; that comprehension IS contact.

**The ending.** It hands the last word back: you compose the final utterance from the shared vocabulary. Whatever you say, it answers with the first word you ever confirmed together. Circle closed.

---

## Pitch 3 — The Break (mandatory-loss pitch)

**The movements.** (1) PLAY: rapport built through games it invents and you complete — chase, mimicry, keep-away. (2) TRUST: it starts giving you things — fragments it made, which live fragile on the screen, movable by you. (3) THE BREAK: a game escalates and your natural winning move — the move the design has been rewarding for ten minutes — shatters what it gave you. Not a trap; a cost. It withdraws. Nothing is narrated. (4) REPAIR: the old games get no answer. The only path back is unrewarded gentleness — tending the broken pieces with the soft early gestures from movement one. Repair is action-driven: each mended fragment re-earns a degree of presence.

**The midpoint turn.** The break itself. The relationship acquires a past.

**The climax.** It speaks for the first time only after repair — and its first utterance references the break, not the friendship. What it finally says is closer to forgiveness than greeting.

**The ending.** It offers you the last fragment — whole — and lets you choose: keep it or give it back. Both endings land; giving it back lands harder.

---

## Pitch 4 — Every Door Is An Exit (mandatory tab-close pitch)

**The structure.** A chain of complete cadences instead of one deferred payoff. The arc is 7–9 small movements, each shaped like a miniature one-act: approach, exchange, rest. Every movement ENDS — a settled image, a held note, a beat of mutual stillness that would read as a real ending if the tab closed there. The escalation is that each cadence is more intimate than the last: ending 1 is "it noticed you," ending 4 is "it played with you," ending 7 is "it spoke."

**The midpoint turn.** Around cadence 4, the rests invert: it stops resting away from you and starts resting toward you — the stillness becomes shared instead of parallel.

**The climax.** The final cadence is the only one it initiates. It performs the whole grammar of approach-exchange-rest at you, and the exchange is speech.

**The ending.** There is no wrong exit. Leave at any rest and you left after a goodbye; the design guarantees you are never more than ~90 seconds of interaction from a cadence. Returning visitors get a warm reprise of their last cadence, never a requirement.

---

## Pitch 5 — The Depth Dive (Frog Fractions shape)

**The movements.** (1) SURFACE: an innocuous, mundane single page — deliberately small. Poking its edges reveals give: something under the page reacts. (2) UNDER: the visitor peels the page (a committed gesture — drag, hold, pry) and finds the inhabited layer; the mundane site was the shell it lives in. Each deeper layer is earned by a new kind of interaction the previous layer taught. (3) DEEP: layers stop being spaces and start being its interior — you are inside its attention.

**The midpoint turn.** Perspective flip: a layer where the thing you've been descending toward is depicted looking UP — you realize you were never exploring down; it was drawing you in.

**The climax.** At the deepest layer it speaks — and what it says reveals the surface page was its first attempt to talk: the mundane site was a sentence you couldn't read yet. The whole session retroactively becomes dialogue.

**The ending.** It carries you back up. The surface page is subtly rewritten in light of everything — same page, now legible. Closing the tab on a page you can finally read is the ending.

---

## FINAL — The Locked Shape

One arc, room-built. Frame: Naturalist's MIMIC — a slightly wrong tide-table page that is the creature holding a posture. The escalation axis is EXPOSURE; the ladder is Naturalist's irreversible word-spends. Movements pinned to Puppeteer's body: Act 1 Startle Economy and Grain (gentleness is the only verb), bridge Keepaway, Act 2 Echo-Then-Ask and Wrong Guess, Act 3 Held Breath. Language spine: Poet's Ostension, with Not-An-Echo's recombination pivot as the Act 2-to-3 turn. Cadence law from my Every Door Is An Exit: seven to nine complete rests, each a graded Held Breath (Puppeteer's curve), never more than ninety seconds away; blur or lock resumes at the last rest. Vocabulary ruling (Naturalist/Poet): pre-midpoint words are repossessable by startle; post-midpoint spends are permanent gaps. Midpoint, per Skeptic: use, not recap. The Break: Puppeteer's carried-gift collision fires a half-failed re-camouflage — the reflex meets permanent gaps and flickers. The wound is sensitization (Naturalist); repair is Act 1 verbs re-habituating the threshold, action-bounded, legible within two touches, above Skeptic's responsiveness floor. Climax: speech as final exposure, understood cold. Ending: Skeptic's It Says Goodbye First; the visitor composes, it answers "slack" (Poet); the page rests, gaps unhealed. Screenshot test governs.
