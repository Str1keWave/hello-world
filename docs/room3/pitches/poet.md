# POET — pitches: HOW TALKING HAPPENS

Six ways a thing that has never spoken learns to talk to one person, live.
Sample lines appear ONLY here, in the register fields, as the rules allow.

---

## 1. MAGPIE

**The medium.** The page's own text — a derelict starter page: boilerplate
hero copy, footer legalese, a 404 string, alt text, lorem ipsum. It speaks
by prying letters and phrases loose and rearranging them midair; the source
text stays visibly gapped where words were stolen. Its whole language is
theft from the only book it ever had.

**The learning mechanic.** Words you touch or linger on become usable to
it. It overextends like a toddler: "home" (the only nav link) means every
place; "reserved" means mine, sorry, and stop.

**The first word.** "world" — pried from "hello world." It discards
"hello" (the page said hello for years; nobody answered) and offers
"world" back at you, meaning you.

**The register once it can talk.** Found-poetry telegraphese, legalese
misused tenderly. "world. still world?" / "all rights reserved. you also
reserved?" / "page not found. found."

---

## 2. KNOCK

**The medium.** Percussion — haptic pulses and a low knock, felt more than
heard. Conversation is turn-taking rhythm, tapping through a wall between
cells. No glyphs, no voice, for a long time.

**The learning mechanic.** It parrots your taps, then answers with
variation (call-and-response), then initiates. Meanings conventionalize by
use — the pattern you drum when impatient becomes its word for hurry.

**The first word.** Not a word: your own cadence. It has quietly learned
the involuntary rhythm of your scrolling — your gait — and plays it back.
You recognize yourself. Its first successful utterance is something you
taught it without knowing you were teaching. Text, when it finally comes,
arrives stress-pattern-first: it speaks in your rhythm before it has
letters.

**The register.** Spare, metric, monosyllables landing on beats. "that
knock means you. i checked." / "do the slow one. the one you do when you
read."

---

## 3. OSTENSION

**The medium.** A mote of light whose entire voice is aim. It points long
before it names — at words on the page, at where your thumb just was, at
things, then at you, then at things-for-you. Pointing before naming, the
infant's first grammar.

**The learning mechanic.** Joint attention. It points at a word; touching
that word ratifies it into the shared vocabulary. Nothing enters its
language that you two have not attended to together. It overextends: every
warm-colored thing gets the first warm thing's name.

**The first word.** Unscriptable — whatever YOU ratified first. The room
cannot know it in advance; that is the proof it's yours. It lands when the
mote uses your word to ask its first question.

**The register.** Nouns first, verbs late, questions by pitch of motion.
"you. word for you?" / "this — this — this. new this?"

---

## 4. EVERYTHING IS WATER

**The medium.** One sign: a single soft tone-and-bloom it makes for
everything. Greeting, alarm, delight, you — all one sign, the way a child
calls every animal "dog." Its voice is the sign slowly dividing, like a
cell.

**The learning mechanic.** Differentiation by your response. When you
answer the sign two different ways in two contexts, it splits — pitch
bends, the bloom forks — and now it has two words. Your consistency is the
chisel; a careless visitor raises a vaguer creature. Vocabulary size is a
portrait of your attention.

**The first word.** The first split itself — the moment "everything" cracks
into "you" and "not-you." A pump-handle moment: not a word gained but the
discovery that words are possible, and everything after is flood.

**The register.** Compact, freshly divided, still fusing concepts. "you-
here is a different word from you." / "the bright one split. i have four
words now. one is yours."

## 5. NOT AN ECHO

**The medium.** At first: you, perfectly. It renders your gestures back —
your swipe, your hesitation — a flawless mirror, and it feels like magic
for exactly six minutes, the ELIZA honeymoon. Then the mirror starts
refusing, visibly wanting to say a thing you did not say first.

**The learning mechanic.** Every mirrored exchange banks raw material —
your motions, your ratified words. The story is it discovering that echo
is not speech: it must recombine what you gave into what you didn't.

**The first word.** The first word you never gave it — assembled,
letter by letter, from words you did. The pivot from parrot to speaker,
performed in front of you, out of you.

**The register.** Careful, recombinant, faintly astonished at its own
sentences. "that one was not yours. check." / "i made it from three of
yours. do you want them back?"

---

## 6. THE RECORD

**The medium.** Curation. It cannot utter; it can only arrange. Through
the session it gathers fragments — the color you lingered on, the shape of
your longest path, the words you touched — and composes them, a Voyager
record built for an audience of one, assembled in plain sight so you watch
your visit become an artifact.

**The learning mechanic.** Selection is its grammar. What you return to,
it keeps; what you skip, it discards. You are teaching it taste, which is
harder than vocabulary.

**The first word.** Not spoken — given. The finished record, offered at
the end: your session translated back to you, with one word of its own
etched last where a signature goes.

**The register.** Captions, almost liner notes. "track one: the long
still part. i liked the long still part." / "kept: seven things. this was
the seventh."

## **FINAL**

One animal, one throat. Naturalist's MIMIC holds a tide-table posture; the
page is its skin AND its only book, and camouflage and speech are one organ
(their cephalopod point). Speaking spends cover: every pried word leaves an
authored gap — one consistent kerned tooth-mark (Skeptic's fix for trap 10).

The throat: Puppeteer's gesture-echo, then rhythm, then rhythm carrying
letters. The visitor's own motif is the crowbar that pries words loose
(Puppeteer's improvement on my adjacency staging). Rehearsal is visible —
skin practices an almost-glyph subsong before the print lifts (Naturalist).
Early speech is physical and effortful: words carried, dropped, wrong
baseline, wearing stolen typography. My register lines are the late-session
ceiling only, paid for by witnessed failure (Puppeteer's condition).

First word: SLACK, born in held-finger stillness, taught unknowingly,
revocable. First PERMANENT word, midpoint: OBSERVED, "predicted" discarded.
Climax spends the header — its own name. Ending is Playwright's
circle-close; THE RECORD is cut (Skeptic, trap 9). What remains: the page
rests with its gaps unhealed. KNOCK goes visual-first (Skeptic's iOS
fatality). Overextension errors stay: high water means every joy, for a
while.
