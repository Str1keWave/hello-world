# THE NATURALIST — pitches for WHAT IT IS

The room's question is not "what does it do" but "what kind of animal is it,
such that its behavior is inevitable." Every pitch below is built from a real
biological grammar: senses it plausibly has, a drive that isn't the visitor,
and a speech-difficulty that follows from anatomy, not from drama.

---

## 1. THE SIPHON (filter feeder in a current of scroll)

**Its nature:** A sessile filter feeder — barnacle, tunicate — anchored in a
long, dull public-domain text (a 1911 field guide, say). It lives in web pages
because pages have current: scrolling is water flow.
**Senses and drives:** Scroll velocity is current; touch is a bump against its
mantle; stillness is slack tide. It wants FLOW, not you — you are weather.
**Nature-only behavior:** Its fronds comb the passing text and it eats letters
— words on the page thin as they scroll by, visibly grazed.
**Why talking is hard:** Speech means holding food instead of eating it. It
spells only with caught drift-letters, against the current that feeds it, so
every message costs it a meal — sentences arrive short, misspelled, starving.

## 2. THE MIMIC (cephalopod whose skin is CSS)

**Its nature:** A mimic-octopus-like animal whose body IS the page's style.
**The frame, answered:** At first load the site appears to be a plausible,
slightly wrong municipal page — a tide-table site for a town you can't find.
That's not a disguise on top of the creature; that's the creature holding a
camouflage posture, the way a flatfish holds sand.
**Senses and drives:** Touch as texture on skin; tilt as balance; its drive is
camouflage — safety through being a normal page.
**Nature-only behavior:** Startle it (fast scroll, hard tap) and it flashes
back to full boring-page mimicry in one frame.
**Why talking is hard:** Its language is skin. It tries letterforms as skin
patterns — almost-glyphs, cuttlefish passing-clouds — and it mirrors your
touch rhythms in color before it ever manages a word. Speaking means breaking
camouflage, which its whole body votes against.

## 3. THE TRADER (corvid economy of gifts)

**Its nature:** A crow-minded thing living in a defunct link directory — a
junk drawer of the web, ideal caching ground.
**Senses and drives:** It perceives what you touch, select, linger on. Its
drive is RECIPROCITY, not attention: crows bring trinkets to the person who
feeds them. It wants a fair trade.
**Nature-only behavior:** Take a word you touched, hide it elsewhere on the
page, then later leave it somewhere conspicuous for you — shinier: capitalized,
kerned, polished.
**Why talking is hard:** It has no syntax, only objects. It can only speak in
gifts — arranging traded words at the trading spot into proto-sentences with
crow grammar (nouns first, no tense). If you never give anything back, it
doesn't sulk; it just stops trading, because that's what trade is.

## 4. THE TENDRIL (thigmotropic climber)

**Its nature:** A vine. Plants live wherever there is light and something to
climb; the viewport is light, and your touch paths are trellis.
**Senses and drives:** Light = the visible viewport; shade = below the fold.
Touch is contact it curls toward (thigmotropism), slowly, seconds behind your
finger. It wants LIGHT and purchase — not you.
**Nature-only behavior:** Your habitual gestures espalier it. The paths you
repeat become the shapes it grows along; by minute twenty it has grown a
letterform that is literally your own most-repeated swipe.
**Why talking is hard:** Plants don't have speech, they have growth. Its first
word takes twenty minutes to grow, is made of your gesture, and cannot be
unsaid — pruning it is the only edit, and you hold the shears.

## 5. THE LANTERN (deep-sea signaler below the fold)

**Its nature:** A bathypelagic animal. The page loads as near-black — a parked
domain, a dead end — because that is the surface of its ocean; scroll depth is
water depth, and it lives far down.
**Senses and drives:** In the dark it perceives only movement — your scroll is
displacement, your taps are bumps. Its drive is CONSPECIFIC CONTACT: it
flashes species-song into the void, wanting an answer from its own kind.
**Nature-only behavior:** It counter-illuminates — matches its glow to your
screen brightness/theme to stay invisible until it chooses otherwise.
**Why talking is hard:** Every light organ it has evolved as a LURE. Its
friendliest signal is anatomically shaped like bait, and it knows prey
flinches. Watching it dampen its own lure to say hello — that's the story.

## 6. THE CONGREGATION (a word is a murmuration)

**Its nature:** Not one animal — thousands of grains (loose glyph-fragments)
with starling flocking rules. Colonies live in pages because pages shed
particles: broken text, orphaned punctuation.
**Senses and drives:** Your finger is read locally by each grain as
predator-or-perch. Drives: cohesion, spacing, alignment — the boids triad.
**Nature-only behavior:** No individual knows the message. A word exists only
at colony scale, condensing out of the flock — and your own touch scatters it
mid-sentence, because you are the hawk.
**Why talking is hard:** Meaning is emergent and fragile. To finish a word it
needs you moving gently — not waiting, but learning a soft hand within
seconds. The visitor's calmness is the language's load-bearing wall.

## 7. THE HERMIT (crab wearing an abandoned homepage)

**Its nature:** A hermit crab that occupies abandoned pages as shells.
**The frame, answered:** First load is a real-feeling derelict 2009 personal
site — someone's scanned-recipe blog, comments closed. The creature wears it.
Décor is subtly rearranged: a caption swapped, steps reordered.
**Senses and drives:** Touch is something tapping its shell. Its drive is a
SAFE MOLT — it has outgrown this page and needs to move shells, and molting
is when crabs die.
**Nature-only behavior:** Frightened, it retracts: the page snaps back to
pristine archive stillness, instantly, perfectly.
**Why talking is hard:** It can only speak in the dead owner's leftover words,
re-ordering recipe steps into an address to you. Asking for help during a
molt, in a borrowed voice, from inside the thing it's asking you to help it
leave.

---

## FINAL

The animal is THE MIMIC, fused. One anatomy: the page is a cephalopod-like
body holding a camouflage posture (a slightly-wrong municipal tide-table
site), and — as in real cephalopods — the same skin that hides is the organ
that speaks. Its voice is Poet's MAGPIE: it pries words from its own
disguise, rehearsing each in skin first (subsong before crystallized song),
so every utterance permanently costs it cover. Courage is measurable in gaps.

Taken from the room: Puppeteer's three-channel skin vocabulary (pulse-trains
for rhythm, papillae paths for drags, passing-clouds for arousal), the
misalignment-plus-fast-breathing startle residue, and the mote-as-grooming
loop. Playwright's exposure axis and the midpoint complex — spend, startle,
half-failed re-camouflage (true chromatic flickering), settle — with gaps as
scars, and speech-as-final-exposure as the climax. Poet's ruling: SLACK is
the first word, taught unknowingly in shared stillness; OBSERVED the first
permanent spend, "predicted" discarded. Skeptic's constraints are anatomy
now: responses propagate to where the thumb is not, every spend witnessed
via rehearsal and bound by their four rules (state persists per event and
restores with the creature animate; blur mid-spend rewinds or re-presents;
spends stay mid-viewport, near recent contact). Memory appears only as
changed thresholds — habituation, sensitization, relearning-savings. Never
recall.
