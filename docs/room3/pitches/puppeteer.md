# PUPPETEER — How It Moves and Plays

Six pitches. My lane is pre-verbal: no words from the entity anywhere below.
The arc I'm building toward across all six: reflex → attention → imitation →
initiation → a shared language the two of you invented. "Trying to talk"
should be the LAST thing it learns, and it should learn it from the visitor.

---

## 1. The Startle Economy

**What the visitor does:** Anything, at any speed. Fast flicks, sudden taps,
slow strokes, stillness.

**What it does back:** It has a nervous system before it has anything else.
Fast input makes it contract, dart, go small and tight in a corner. Slow
input lets it unfurl. Stillness makes it curious — it drifts toward the
last touch point, hesitates, retreats, returns closer.

**Why alive:** Programmed things respond uniformly. Creatures have arousal
states. The visitor discovers, without being told, that THEIR body language
matters — they start moving gently the way you lower your voice around a
cat. The site changes how the visitor behaves. That is the proof of life.

**Escalation:** The startle threshold visibly relaxes as trust builds. Late
session, a fast fling makes it flinch — then deliberately un-flinch.

---

## 2. Echo, Then Ask

**What the visitor does:** Draws or taps anything — a squiggle, a
three-tap rhythm, a circle.

**What it does back:** A beat later it performs the gesture back —
imperfectly, in its own body: your drawn circle becomes a wobbly swim in a
loop, your tap-tap-tap becomes three pulses of light. Then, crucially, it
waits, oriented at you. Later it stops copying and starts VARYING: your
rhythm plus one extra beat, offered like a question.

**Why alive:** Imitation-with-error is how infants and parrots converse
before words. Perfect playback is a recorder; a flawed, eager copy is a
student. The variation is the moment it stops mirroring and starts meaning.

**Escalation:** Early: copies. Middle: varies. Late: initiates — performs a
motif YOU invented an hour of gestures ago, unprompted, and waits.

---

## 3. Keepaway (the play loop)

**What the visitor does:** Nothing they were told to do. A loose glowing
mote drifts near the entity. The visitor, inevitably, pokes or drags it.

**What it does back:** It wants the mote. Drag the mote away and it gives
chase — overshooting, banking like a fish. Let the mote go and it noses the
mote back toward your thumb. Push it toward the entity slowly and it feints
disinterest, then pounces. Fling it and the entity catches — or misses.

**Why alive:** No rules screen, no score. The game exists only because both
parties keep choosing to continue — which is the actual definition of play.
Fetch was never taught to any dog either.

**Escalation:** It invents rule changes: hides the mote, offers it back,
starts a possession-swap turn structure — the first grammar.

---

## 4. The Wrong Guess (fallibility)

**What the visitor does:** Establishes any pattern — always stroking left,
always tapping twice, always feeding the mote to the same corner.

**What it does back:** It anticipates — and overcommits. Moves to where the
mote usually goes before you throw it. When you break the pattern, it
arrives triumphantly at the wrong place, holds the pose one beat too long,
then does the smallest possible slink back. Later it double-checks: starts
toward the guess, stops, looks back at your thumb.

**Why alive:** Prediction error is the signature of a mind that models you.
A thing that is never wrong was never guessing. The slink — the visible
cost of the mistake, the tiny embarrassment — is pure body language, and
it is funnier and more endearing than any success.

**Escalation:** Its guesses get genuinely good, so its rare misses land
harder — and it starts baiting YOU into wrong guesses. A joke, pre-verbal.

---

## 5. Grain

**What the visitor does:** Strokes it. Long slow drags across its body.

**What it does back:** It has a grain, like fur or a cat's back. Stroked one
way it ripples and leans into the finger, deforming toward the touch,
trailing afterglow. Stroked against the grain it shudders and compresses.
It has places: one region makes it slow and dopey, another makes it twitch
away every time — a permanent no, never explained, never negotiated.

**Why alive:** Preference without justification is animal. The lean-in is
the key frame: pressure back against your finger, the thing a cat does that
no interface has ever done. The permanent no-spot is the boundary that
makes consent legible — you can't pet all of it, so touching it means something.

**Escalation:** Late session, it initiates: drifts under a resting idle
thumb and strokes ITSELF along it.

---

## 6. Held Breath

**What the visitor does:** Nothing. Holds one finger down and keeps still —
the hardest gesture on a phone, and the only one that costs the visitor
patience measured in seconds, not sessions.

**What it does back:** Its idle breathing gradually synchronizes with a slow
visible swell under the held finger — then it approaches the stillness the
way animals approach anything motionless: closer than it has ever come.
Full contact happens here first, not during play.

**Why alive:** Every creature treats stillness as safety. Rewarding
non-interaction inverts every website instinct the visitor has, and it
makes intimacy something you HOLD rather than something you tap. This is
where the eventual attempt to talk should begin — close up, breath matched,
in the quiet the visitor chose to make.

**Escalation:** Early stillness earns approach. Late stillness earns the
entity resting its whole weight against the finger — and, at the very end,
its first deliberate utterance-shaped act, built from motifs from pitch 2.

---

### Note to the room

These six are one body. Startle economy is the autonomic layer, Grain is
touch, Keepaway is play, Wrong Guess is cognition, Echo is language, Held
Breath is intimacy. Any fiction, tone, or ending the room chooses can sit
on top — but the mandate's whole sentence ("alive, sees you, eventually
trying to talk") is executable in body language alone, and every trap in
the brief is a trap of WORDS. Whatever the entity is, I want its first
attempt at speech to be a rhythm the visitor taught it without either of
them noticing.
